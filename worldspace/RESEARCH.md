# WORLDSPACE — research-derived design hypotheses

Kept apart from repository-derived facts (those live in `manifest.yaml` and
`films/data/facts.json`). Each line below is a hypothesis about how people
read an interactive proof, with the source it was derived from and the
design constraint it produced. None of it is evidence that WORLDSPACE works;
the cold test in `manifest.yaml → cold_test` is where that evidence would come
from. Sources were read for constraints, not aesthetics; no visual style was
borrowed.

| id | hypothesis | derived from | constraint it produced |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Eliciting a prediction before revelation improves recall and comprehension; the *gap* between prediction and outcome is the learning signal, and visual predictions beat textual ones. | Kim, Reinecke & Hullman, *Explaining the Gap* (CHI 2017); Hullman, Kay, Kim & Shrestha, *Imagining Replications* (TVCG 2018) | Act I commits a number before anything moves; Act III places it as a coordinate on the identified axis, never scored, never shamed. |
| H2 | Animated transitions help viewers track objects when identity is preserved, occlusion is minimal and trajectories are predictable; staggering elements adds little. | Heer & Robertson, *Animated Transitions in Statistical Data Graphics* (InfoVis 2007); Chevalier, Dragicevic & Franconeri, *The Not-so-Staggering Effect…* (TVCG 2014) | One ring moves at a time, as one object, along a straight path the visitor chose; no cascades, no staggered reveals. |
| H3 | Animation fails the apprehension principle when it is too fast or complex to perceive; interactivity that lets the viewer pace the change repairs it; the moving thing must be the thing that changes conceptually (congruence). | Tversky, Morrison & Bétrancourt, *Animation: can it facilitate?* (IJHCS 2002) | Direct manipulation instead of autoplay; the only animated object is the overlap, because the overlap is the only quantity that changes. |
| H4 | Extraneous material harms learning (coherence, median d ≈ 0.86 across 23 tests; seductive details g ≈ −0.4); cues that direct attention help (signalling, g ≈ 0.38). | Mayer & Fiorella, coherence/signalling chapter, *Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning*; Schneider et al. signalling meta-analysis (ETR&D 2020) | Nothing decorative on the stage; the invariant is *signalled* in words once it becomes true — THE SCORES DIDN'T MOVE. — because a non-change is not noticed on its own. |
| H5 | Working memory limits visual comparison to a few items per second; animation engages but can confuse; ensemble statistics are fast, item comparisons are slow. | Franconeri, Padilla, Shah, Zacks & Hullman, *The Science of Visual Data Communication* (PSPI 2021) | One dominant question, one dominant object, one meaningful change per state; readouts adjacent to the object that changes. |
| H6 | Curiosity follows an information gap opened by a violated expectation; a committed expectation makes the violation legible. | Loewenstein, information-gap theory (Psych. Bull. 1994) and the appraisal literature that followed | PREDICT → COMMIT → OBSERVE → SURPRISE → EXPLAIN → VERIFY, in that order; the explanation never precedes the surprise. |
| H7 | Visualizations memorable at a glance tend to convey their message; titles and short text carry the message; redundancy of message helps recognition. | Borkin et al., *What Makes a Visualization Memorable?* (TVCG 2013); *Beyond Memorability* (TVCG 2015) | A few exact words at the moments that matter (SAME SCORES. DIFFERENT WORLD. — ONE ASSUMPTION. ONE POINT.), not paragraphs. |
| H8 | Visuals and navigation feedback affect engagement; discrete versus continuous control may not. | McKenna, Henry Riche, Lee, Boy & Meyer, *Visual Narrative Flow* (EuroVis 2017) | Discrete counts (0 … 10) are acceptable and exact; every state change gives visible feedback. |
| H9 | Directness is small semantic and articulatory distance plus engagement — acting on the object itself. | Hutchins, Hollan & Norman, *Direct Manipulation Interfaces* (HCI 1985) | Drag the ring onto the disc; the count is a consequence of the act, not a parameter typed elsewhere. A range input remains as the non-drag path. |
| H10 | Motion animation triggered by interaction must be disableable unless essential; a reduced-motion preference must be honoured; pointer targets ≥ 24 CSS px (AA), dragging needs a single-pointer alternative. | WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3, 2.5.7, 2.5.8; W3C technique C39 | Every transition is a state change under `prefers-reduced-motion`; a range input and tap-to-hop replace dragging; targets are the cell (≥ 32 px at 390 px width). |
| H11 | Drawing a distribution shape over an interval invites readers to treat the interval as an estimate's uncertainty. | Hofman, Goldstein & Hullman, *How Visualizing Inferential Uncertainty Can Mislead…* (CHI 2020); Padilla, Kay & Hullman, uncertainty-visualization review | The field shows the same number of specimens at every position (attainment), never a histogram; the counting-measure fact is stated in words one interaction deeper. |
| H12 | Audio cannot start before a user gesture and must be opt-in; sound can carry structure when it encodes a variable. | Chrome and Safari autoplay policies; Web Audio API documentation | A single SOUND OFF / SOUND ON button; pitch encodes the joint count; nothing plays until pressed; silent comprehension is mandatory. |
| H13 | Progressive and staged disclosure reduce initial load; later steps are required for completion. | Nielsen, progressive disclosure (1995; NN/g) | Six acts revealed in sequence; boundaries and provenance one interaction deeper; the exit is staged behind the field. |
| H14 | The compatible joint laws of two Bernoulli marginals form a one-parameter segment between the countermonotone and comonotone couplings; every point is attained. | Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds; the 2×2 fixed-margins polytope | The axis *is* the identified set; the endpoint witnesses are its vertices; the visitor's world is a point on it. |
| H15 | A uniform prior over all arrangements compatible with the scorecard has mean overlap exactly 10·10/100 = 1 — the independence point is the counting measure's mean. | Hypergeometric arithmetic on the constructed population (checked in `generate_worldspace.py`) | Independence is presented as *a choice of measure over worlds*, not as an error; the fact is stated one interaction deeper, never drawn. |

What these hypotheses do **not** license: any claim that WORLDSPACE teaches
better than the film, the figure, or a paragraph. That comparison is the next
experiment.
