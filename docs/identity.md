**150 words**

> Pranav Bhave is an AI assurance researcher working on guardrail composition,
> statistical bounds, and evidence engineering. His flagship result is simple
> to state: guardrail evaluations publish a score for each detector and almost
> never publish what the stack misses together — and that missing number cannot
> be recovered from the published ones. Marginal miss rates identify a joint
> all-miss rate only up to an interval, and independence is one point inside it
> rather than the answer.
>
> He maintains the Missing Column Census, a bounded, source-bound inventory of
> public guardrail evaluations with a live correction route, and proposes the
> Minimum Joint Guardrail Disclosure: union detection and all-miss rate over one
> denominator, with a tested reference implementation. His public record binds
> every claim to a falsifier, records its own corrections, and re-checks itself
> in CI. He studies computer science at Penn State.

**Speaker bio**

> Pranav Bhave researches what AI guardrail stacks miss together. He maintains

> evaluations preserve joint evidence, and which leave the stack's own behaviour
> unmeasured — and proposes a minimum disclosure that fixes it in one table row.
> His public research record carries a falsifier for every claim and a public
> log of its own corrections, including the ones that rejected his earlier
> counts.

## Profile image strategy

One photograph, everywhere: the existing portrait, square-cropped to the face
and shoulders. Same crop and same source file on the site, X, LinkedIn, and
GitHub, so the same person is recognisable at 32 px in a reply thread and at
full width on the homepage. No illustrated avatar, no logo in the avatar slot —
the campaign object is the *banner* and the *social card*, never the face.

## Project description hierarchy

Use this order and this wording wherever the programs are listed.

1. **The Missing Column** — the finding, the census, and the disclosure. The
   thing people should remember.
2. **CC-Framework** — the composition mathematics and claim-boundary machinery
   underneath it.
3. **Ghost-Ark** — provenance and what a receipt can and cannot identify.
4. *Supporting experiments*: Ghost Visualizer (communication, under
   CC-Framework) and Assay (provenance, under Ghost-Ark). Named as experiments,
   never as peer programs.
5. **The record itself** — described by what it does for a reader ("evidence
   infrastructure: how this site keeps claims bound to sources, review
   triggers, and public corrections"), never by its own metaphor.
