## NR-001 — The evidence-surface identification transplant does not work

**Date:** 2026-08-28 · **Verdict:** dropped · **Cost:** one derivation-and-refutation pass (8 adversarial lenses over 4 claims)

### What was proposed

An assurance extension of Anthropic's AI Fluency framework. Four evidence
surfaces — `O` outcome, `P` process trace, `R` reflective self-report, `W`
external witness — treated as *marginals* of a latent competence `C`, with
the research question: **which additional evidence surface reduces the
identification width the most?** The appeal was obvious: it appeared to
transplant the Fréchet result in [`identification.py`](../scripts/identification.py)
into a human-assessment domain, giving the assurance work a natural human side.

### Why it fails

**The target is ill-posed, which is strictly worse than partially
identified.** Partial identification presupposes a well-defined estimand the
data fails to pin down. `P(all-miss)` is perfectly well defined and only the
coupling is unknown. "Diligence" was never defined as a functional of
anything — no population, no unit set, no cutpoint on the continuum from
skimmed to independently re-derived. Upgrading an ill-posed target to a
partially identified one is exactly what made the two problems look
isomorphic.

**`O`, `P`, `R` are not marginals.** A marginal is a lossless exact
projection; none of the three is that. And "attained" degrades from *realised
by an admissible coupling* to *not contradicted by the evidence* — under which
every unrefuted hypothesis is "attained." The word does no work and carries
borrowed credit. What is left of the analogy is "the image of a box under a
monotone map is an interval," true of any monotone functional on any product
order.

**The analogy generated false statements that this repository's own tests
refute.** That is the decisive evidence it was not load-bearing:

| Claim generated by the analogy | Refuted by |
|---|---|
| "Exactly one endpoint is fixed; the other carries no evidential content" | [`identification.py`](../scripts/identification.py) ships `p=(.9,.9) → [0.8, 0.9]` as a regression test — an evidence-bearing lower endpoint |
| "Conjunctive functionals certified from above, disjunctive from below" | `burden_interval` returns a **two-sided** bound on a disjunction |
| "The independence plug-in's interiority is a theorem, so R has no such guarantee" | The plug-in is compatible, not necessarily interior — sometimes at an endpoint |

An analogy whose errors are caught by the test suite of the thing it is
analogising to is not a theorem being extended. It is a source of error
wearing borrowed authority.

**The rest is familiar.** You cannot infer a negative from a non-exclusive
channel. Unverifiable self-report is unverifiable. Ratios with actor-chosen
denominators are gameable. These are true, and they are not discoveries —
negation-as-failure under a closed-world assumption is a database result from
the 1970s; the metric critique is Goodhart with arithmetic attached.

### One specific error worth keeping

**A resolved citation is not a witness.** It was listed as one. Citation
resolution is a standing property of the world, evaluable by the assessor, by
the model at generation time, or by nobody — it is causally independent of any
act of checking. Counting resolutions into a verification floor makes the
floor *unsound*, not merely loose. A witness certifies a human epistemic act
only if it is **act-costly** (producible only by performing the act) and
**identity-bound** (attributable to this human rather than the model or the
toolchain). Most items on the obvious list of witnesses fail one or both.

### The one thing worth carrying forward

Argued, not measured — treat it as a hypothesis, not a result:

> **Every witness an assurance apparatus can scale is invariant to which agent
> performed the act.** Resolved citations, green tests, commits, and matching
> hashes are equally producible by the model. So the only channel that raises
> measured diligence at scale is precisely the channel an assistant saturates
> at near-zero cost.

If true, it predicts that witness-density metrics lose validity monotonically
as assistants improve, and that an assessor who follows the natural advice
("make witnesses cheap to emit and expensive to fake") accelerates the
decoupling rather than arresting it — building telemetry that measures the
assistant and reports it as the human's floor. This is checkable and matters
for anyone designing AI-era assessment. It is also a direct consequence of
"artifact ≠ act," which a careful reader already holds.

### What was kept instead

The one part of the excursion that produced a real, reproducible result was
much smaller and had nothing to do with latent competence:
[`mixture_bounds.py`](../scripts/mixture_bounds.py) and claim AF-001 — a bound
on what the AI Fluency Index's *published summary* leaves undetermined. That
is arithmetic on stated figures, and it holds.

### Process note

All four derivations returned `correct_with_correction` while both adversarial
lenses returned `refuted` on every one. The aggregate verdict was not supported
by its own record. On the subject of how evidence fails to support claims about
diligence, that is the most self-refuting error in the set — and it is the
argument for running the refutation pass before writing anything down, not
after.

### Scheduling verdict

The excursion arrived with a 30-day, 4.5-hour-a-day curriculum programme
attached. It collides head-on with Days 1–30 of
[FRONTIER_ROADMAP.md](FRONTIER_ROADMAP.md) (corrective census release, then
MJGD v1) and would be a second full-time programme starting the same week as
the first. It is also a poor trade on its own terms: the central insight it was
meant to yield — that 11 of 24 behaviours are observable and the unobservable
13 include the consequential ones — is stated in the report's own limitations
section and took one web fetch to confirm. **Not run.**
