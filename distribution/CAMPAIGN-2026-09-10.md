## LessWrong — a packet, not a draft

**The post must be written by the owner, in the owner's hand.** LessWrong's
audience is specifically good at detecting assembled prose, the norm there treats
authorship as load-bearing, and a post about epistemic honesty that was not
written by its byline is self-refuting. So what follows is the packet: the spine,
the numbers, the objections and the answers. **Not paste-ready text, deliberately.**

**Working title.** *The joint doesn't survive summarization.*

**The spine, five beats.**
1. The theorem, in one paragraph, with the guardrail example. Credit
   arXiv:2607.22868 for the any-flag statement in the same paragraph, not in a
   footnote.
2. The empirical beat: E7B. Preregistered, public data, 15 pairs, all five held,
   7 at the ceiling. State n=21 in the same breath as the result.
3. The void beat: E7. This is the paragraph that buys the rest of the post. Do
   not soften it and do not bury it below the fold.
4. The general beat: E6. Not "Anthropic was wrong" — "a careful, heavily reviewed
   team computed the joint correctly, stated the distinction in Appendix B, and
   the distinction still did not reach the abstract." The claim is about
   *summarization*, not about them.
5. The ask, and what would change our mind. Both falsifiers, explicitly.

**The strongest objections, and what actually answers them.**
- *"You're just rediscovering Fréchet bounds."* Yes. Concede it in the first
  paragraph and cite Embrechts et al., *An Academic Response to Basel 3.5* (Risks
  2(1):25–48, 2014), whose Table 4 gives sharp best- and worst-case 99.9% VaR of
  **31 to 465** for eight Pareto(2) risks with every marginal known exactly. The
  contribution is measurement, not theory.
- *"The model doesn't assume independence — those are conditionals."* Correct, and
  it is the sharpest objection. Raise it yourself before a commenter does. The
  adoption dial asks what share of the tasks AI *can* do people will use it for,
  so the product within one respondent is the chain rule and is exact. E6 varies
  the coupling *across* the 10,980 respondents. No chain rule fixes that.
- *"n=21 is nothing."* Also correct. Say so, give the step size (0.048), and note
  that 15 pairs from 6 judges are not 15 independent observations.
- *"This is a hit piece on Anthropic."* Answered by structure, not by protest:
  E7B leads, the credit to Ray is in the theorem paragraph, and the Anthropic
  section opens with what they did right and closes with a one-row ask.

**Citations that must appear.** arXiv:2607.22868v1 (the bound, the phenomenon, the
data). Embrechts et al. 2014 (marginals known exactly, answer still unfixed).
Anthropic Institute WP 2026-02 (Table 2, Table 4, Appendix B). Dung & Mai,
arXiv:2510.11235 (states the alignment-technique version: 0.1¹⁰ under
independence versus 0.1 under perfect correlation, and notes the question has had
no dedicated treatment) — this is the closest prior work in AI safety and must be
cited, not discovered by a commenter.

**One live hook worth answering directly.** *When can we trust untrusted
monitoring? A safety case sketch across collusion strategies* (LessWrong
2026-03-10, LASR Labs, supervised at Oxford / UK AISI) states in its own
limitations: "Our statistical modelling assumed trusted and untrusted monitor
scores are independent, which likely overestimates safety." They wrote the gap
down and left it open. Closing someone's declared limitation is the most
respected move available in that venue, and it is a comment on their post before
it is a post of our own.
